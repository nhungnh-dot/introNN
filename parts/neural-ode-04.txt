trajectory}+\text{observed trajectory}
        \longrightarrow \text{loss}
        \]
      </div>

      <p class="small">
        A small loss means the predicted trajectory is close to the observed one.
        A large loss means the model still needs to improve.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Training step 6 · Backpropagation finds the gradients</div>

      <p class="small">
        The loss tells us how good the prediction is, but it does not tell us how to change the neural network.
        Backpropagation provides that information.
      </p>

      <div class="card center math-cell">
        \[
        \text{loss}\longrightarrow \text{backpropagation}
        \longrightarrow \text{parameter gradients}
        \]
      </div>

      <p class="small">
        These gradients tell us how the weights and biases should change to reduce the loss.
      </p>

      <div class="card panel small">
        <strong>Connection to the earlier lesson:</strong>
        this is still the chain rule.
        The only new feature is that the predicted trajectory was created by repeatedly using the neural network together with an ODE solver.
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Training step 7 · Gradient descent updates the neural network</div>

      <p class="small">
        Gradient descent uses those gradients to update the weights and biases.
      </p>

      <div class="card center math-cell">
        \[
        \text{gradients}\longrightarrow \text{gradient descent}
        \longrightarrow \text{updated parameters}
        \]
      </div>

      <p class="small">
        The updated parameters are sent back to the neural network, and the whole training cycle repeats.
      </p>
    </div>

    <p class="small" style="margin:22px 4px 12px 4px">
      Now that the <strong>Training view</strong> is complete, we follow the <strong>Prediction view</strong> from left to right.
    </p>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Prediction step 1 · Last observed state</div>

      <p class="small">
        After training, we begin from the last observed state
        \(z(T)\).
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Prediction step 2 · Learned neural network</div>

      <p class="small">
        The trained neural network uses the current state to predict the rate of change,
        just as it did during training.
      </p>

      <div class="card center math-cell">
        \[
        (t,z(t))\longrightarrow \text{learned }f_\theta
        \longrightarrow \text{predicted }z'(t)
        \]
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Prediction step 3 · ODE solver continues forward</div>

      <p class="small">
        The ODE solver keeps applying the learned rate-of-change rule for times after
        \(T\).
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Prediction step 4 · Future trajectory</div>

      <p class="small">
        Continuing the learned dynamics gives the predicted future trajectory:
      </p>

      <div class="card center math-cell">
        \[
        \text{last observed state}
        \longrightarrow \text{learned neural network}
        \longrightarrow \text{ODE solver}
        \longrightarrow \text{future trajectory}
        \]
      </div>

      <div class="card panel small">
        <strong>Important:</strong>
        no future points are used in the loss.
        The future is predicted by continuing the learned dynamics beyond the observation interval.
      </div>

      <div class="card panel small">
        <strong>Caution:</strong>
        this is still extrapolation.
        If the learned dynamics are inaccurate, the prediction may become less reliable farther into the future.
      </div>
    </div>

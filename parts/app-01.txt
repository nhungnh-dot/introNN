    <div class="card">
      <div class="small muted">Section 1 · First encounter with a neural network</div>
      <div class="h2">What is a neural network actually doing?</div>
      <p>A neural network can sound mysterious because we often hear about very large models. But its basic building block is simple: <b>take some numbers, combine them, and pass the result through a function.</b></p>
      <p class="small">A large network may contain millions of parameters, but each small computation uses familiar operations: multiplication, addition, and composition of functions.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="card panel">
      <div class="h3">A first mental picture</div>
      <p class="small">Suppose a neuron receives two measurements, \(x_1\) and \(x_2\). It first forms a weighted sum:</p>
      <div class="card center math-cell">\[z=w_1x_1+w_2x_2+b\]</div>
      <p class="small">The weights tell us how strongly the inputs contribute. The bias gives an additional shift.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Why is the weighted sum not the whole neuron?</div>
      <p class="small">If every layer of a neural network only formed weighted sums, then stacking many layers would still produce one overall linear transformation. The network would not gain the nonlinear flexibility needed to model complicated patterns.</p>
      <p class="small">So after computing \(z\), a neuron applies an <b>activation function</b>.</p>
      <div class="card center math-cell">\[x_1,x_2 \longrightarrow z=w_1x_1+w_2x_2+b \longrightarrow y=\sigma(z)\]</div>
    </div>

    <div class="card panel">
      <div class="h3">Activation functions</div>
      <p class="small">An activation function transforms the weighted sum before passing the result forward. Different neural networks use different activation functions. In this lesson, we will use the <b>sigmoid</b> because it is smooth and makes the chain rule especially easy to see.</p>
      <div class="card center math-cell">\[\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}\]</div>
      <p class="small">The sigmoid maps every real number to a value between 0 and 1. Large positive values of \(z\) give outputs near 1, large negative values give outputs near 0, and \(z=0\) gives exactly 0.5.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="card">
      <div class="h3">Explore the sigmoid first</div>
